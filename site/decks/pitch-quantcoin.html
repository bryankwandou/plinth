QuantCoin keeps long-held Solana tokens safe from a quantum computer

A Token-2022 token with a hybrid vault · Bryan Kwandou · devnet, pre-audit

QuantCoin is a Solana token whose treasury, team and reserve balances sit in a vault that needs two signatures to open. One is the normal Ed25519 signature. The other is a hash-based signature that a quantum computer cannot forge. It is on devnet and it has not had an external audit yet.
Problem

Effectively all SOL sits behind keys a quantum computer can break

All SOL

Citi counts over 65% of ETH as exposed. For Solana, where the address is the public key, it counts effectively the whole supply.

Source: Citi Institute, Quantum Threat, January 2026 [E1]

This is Citi's number, not mine. On Solana your address is your Ed25519 public key, so every balance has its key on-chain already. A treasury meant to sit for twenty years can be read today and drained the day the machine exists.
How close

Google cut the machine needed to break these keys twentyfold

~20x

Under 500,000 physical qubits now solve the curve problem behind Ed25519, down from the previous best estimate.

Source: Google Research, March 2026 [R2]

Every new paper makes the attack cheaper. Experts surveyed by the Global Risk Institute put a code-breaking machine within ten years at 28 to 49 percent [T1]. Anza puts it at 3 to 5 percent within five [T4]. I build for the long end, not the short one.
Deadline

NIST disallows EdDSA, Solana's signature family, after 2035

2035

The US, UK and EU all set 2035 as the date to finish moving off quantum-vulnerable cryptography.

Source: NIST IR 8547, November 2024 [G1]; UK NCSC, March 2025 [G4]

A vesting schedule signed today can still be running in 2035. Regulated custodians will have to answer for it before then.
Why now

NIST standardized hash-based signatures in 2024

FIPS 205

SLH-DSA rests only on a hash function. Grover's algorithm gives a square-root speedup against it, nothing more. The conservative choice now has a standard behind it.

Source: NIST, August 2024 [G3]

Hash-based signatures are the oldest and most conservative post-quantum family. Once NIST put its name on them, using the same idea on Solana stopped being exotic.
Product

A vault opens only when both signatures check out

  • Ed25519 from the owner, as in any Solana wallet
  • A Winternitz one-time signature over SHA-256
  • Every spend empties the vault, so no key signs twice
An attacker has to break both. Before quantum computers exist, Ed25519 also covers any bug in the newer Winternitz code. After, the hash half still holds. Everyday transfers stay plain Token-2022, so wallets need nothing new.

How it works

The program stores nothing and fits in 6,872 bytes

6,872 B

Pinocchio, one instruction. The vault address is derived from the Winternitz key and the owner. The signed message binds every account, so a relayer cannot redirect funds.

Source: QuantCoin README, measured numbers

No state means nothing to corrupt. The address itself commits to the keys. Program, vault, mint, destination, refund and amount are all inside the signed message.
